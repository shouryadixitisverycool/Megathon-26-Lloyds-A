# Research paper integration assessment

**Date:** 2026-10-10 · **Branch:** `research-papers-integration` · **Deliverable:** assessment and proposals only; no implementation or regeneration.

## Outcome: strengthen the evidence before adding architecture

**GreenPath already implements substantial research-aligned engineering, but does not reproduce all ten papers.** Its strongest existing foundations are point-in-time customer features, leakage-aware synthetic evaluation, supervised transaction categorisation with rule-first routing, validation-fitted binary calibration, exact local selected-model SHAP, constrained language presentation, and evidence-bound physical scenario calculations. These mechanisms support a credible, safety-conscious hackathon demonstration. The largest missing paper-specific contributions are transaction-derived consumption footprints, transaction-token explanations, hierarchical classification, probabilistic weak supervision, global SHAP analysis, and Explingo-style attribution narration with independent quality grading. Reviews and benchmark studies do not prescribe a mandatory GAN, neural network, or universally superior calibrator. The highest-value next step is a coherent, reproducible evidence bundle and accurate claims, followed by small explanation, calibration, and ranking-evaluation additions. **Every proposed benefit below is prospective, not a measured improvement.** ([Assessment notes](../research_notes/Research%20paper%20integration%20assessment/language_and_leakage.md); [current submission scope](../SUBMISSION.md))

### Four boundaries govern every verdict

**Physical potential savings are not transaction inventories.** Current UK/India methods use physical baseline/scenario evidence, whereas Trendl estimates consumption emissions from monetary multipliers and González-González infers activity from prices. Existing physical evidence does not implement transaction-footprint attribution. Separate results must not be silently added together. ([Physical engine](../src/greenpath/extensions/uk_impact.py), lines 19–162; [impact invariants](../src/greenpath/contracts/impact_detail.py), lines 130–200)

**Routing rules are not weak supervision, and flat labels are not a hierarchy.** A deterministic rule followed by a supervised text model has neither a probabilistic LF aggregation stage nor compatible parent/child predictions. Hierarchy and weak supervision both need realistic inputs and independently reviewed labels; the project's own synthetic truth cannot validate their real-world utility. ([Classifier](../src/greenpath/models/transaction_classifier.py), lines 329–448; [training reference](../src/greenpath/pipelines/run.py), lines 134–155)

**Propensity probability, correctness confidence, Trust, and attribution are different quantities.** SHAP explains raw uncalibrated log-odds, not calibrated probability or carbon. Correctness confidence concerns a thresholded propensity decision, not adoption probability, financial eligibility, or a guaranteed reliability probability. **All four saved core confidence decisions are SUPPRESSED**; runtime code honors persisted decisions, but no running trained deployment was tested. ([SHAP contract](../src/greenpath/models/propensity/shap_audit.py), lines 15–57; [confidence validation](./CONFIDENCE_VALIDATION.md); [runtime loader](../src/greenpath/pipelines/runtime.py), lines 81–145)

**Saved reports are not one certified current-source run.** The notes report 39 absent fitted-artifact paths, 10 absent processed files, 22 absent inputs, and an absent split CSV. Of 37 listed outputs, four hashes differed: `explanation_evaluation.json`, `scenario_evaluation.json`, `system_performance.json`, and `MODEL_CARD_OPTIONAL.md`; 43 listed factor-file hashes matched after resolving package-relative paths. These are prior read-only audit findings, not newly reproduced live bugs. Matching hashes establish byte consistency, not model correctness; mismatches do not establish fraud, leakage, or their historical cause. ([Detailed audit and command](../research_notes/Research%20paper%20integration%20assessment/language_and_leakage.md); [saved manifest](./RUN_MANIFEST.json))

## 1. Trendl et al.: transaction spending does not yet yield a consumption footprint

**Paper:** Anna Trendl, Anne Owen, Lara Vomfell, Lena Kilian, John Gathergood, Neil Stewart, and David Leake, *Estimating carbon footprints from large scale financial transaction data*, Journal of Industrial Ecology 27(1), 56–70, **2023**; online publication December 2022. **Primary:** [DOI/publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jiec.13351); [institutional record](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/169598/). **Supplied text:** [carbon-footprints-from-financial-transactions.md](../research-papers/carbon-footprints-from-financial-transactions.md). **Assessment source:** [carbon note](../research_notes/Research%20paper%20integration%20assessment/carbon.md).

### Actual contribution and verdict

The study links bank transaction categories to UK consumption-based MRIO emissions multipliers. Fine COICOP multipliers have dimensions **kg CO2e/GBP**; one-to-many bank-category mappings use household expenditure weights. Categorized spend multiplied by these factors gives an estimated footprint level, aggregated by consumption domain and person/household. The paper addresses non-consumption exclusions, cash allocation, account completeness, and survey-distribution comparison. It does not propose a product-adoption model or a baseline-minus-intervention savings calculator. ([Supplied methods](../research-papers/carbon-footprints-from-financial-transactions.md), sections 2.3–2.5, lines 316–436)

**Verdict: partial enabling infrastructure; central accounting method not implemented.** GreenPath's classifier and time-windowed spend are useful upstream components, but neither UK physical factors nor recommendation portfolio totals reproduce the paper. The study's proprietary TCS, sample rules, and supplementary mapping are not universal requirements or available numerical artifacts in this checkout.

### Implemented aspects and missing mechanisms

`TransactionCategoriser` normalizes descriptions and combines boundary/brand rules with word/character TF-IDF logistic regression. `_transaction_features` calculates debit spend over 30/90/365-day windows; `build_customer360` uses event and availability cutoffs. No-history NaN versus observed-history zero is a valuable distinction. ([Classifier](../src/greenpath/models/transaction_classifier.py), lines 137–245, 284–448; [Customer360](../src/greenpath/pipelines/customer360.py), lines 91–161, 317–354)

Missing are a COICOP consumption taxonomy, reviewed monetary factors, weighted category mappings, consumption exclusions/refund/transfer accounting, cash and household allocation, coverage-qualified footprint totals, and compatible survey validation. Eight recommendation-oriented categories collapse food/services/retail into OTHER and bundled supplies into UTILITIES. Debit-only filtering does not establish consumption eligibility; `txn_months_observed` is elapsed span, not activity in every month or primary-bank completeness. ([Enum](../src/greenpath/contracts/enums.py), lines 34–42; [raw schema](../src/greenpath/pipelines/schemas.py), lines 72–102; [portfolio arithmetic](../src/greenpath/api/portfolio.py), lines 21–51)

Current physical inputs produce signed operational differences with evidence passports and unavailable/null behavior. The UK code pins **DESNZ 2026 physical factors**, not MRIO monetary multipliers. This implemented parallel capability should remain intact rather than be rewritten to satisfy a different paper. ([UK engine](../src/greenpath/extensions/uk_impact.py), lines 64–162; [2026 catalogue](../src/greenpath/extensions/uk_factors_2026.json); [impact contract](../src/greenpath/contracts/impact_detail.py), lines 130–200)

### Small staged plan, with larger dependencies explicit

1. **Separate the result contract first.** Propose `contracts/footprint.py` with observed period/account scope, currency/price year, COICOP domains, counted/excluded/unmapped spend, method/mapping/factor versions, kg CO2e, coverage, and limitations. A footprint level must not pass as `ImpactResult` savings or enter existing recommendation totals.
2. **Acquire and review factors before arithmetic.** Propose `impact/consumption_mapping.py` and a versioned monetary catalogue under `impact/factors/`. Pin source bytes, units, price/tax boundary, year, currency, direct/indirect scope, and mapping weights. Compute `m_k = emissions_k / spend_k`; reject nonpositive denominators and invalid weights. Leave OTHER unmapped rather than inventing a universal factor.
3. **Build a narrow original-transaction pipeline.** Propose `pipelines/consumption.py`, extending [schemas.py](../src/greenpath/pipelines/schemas.py) only through coordinated contract changes for linked refunds/transfers and account/household coverage. Apply event/ingestion cutoffs; distinguish purchases, repayments, transfers, credits, fees, and unknown cash. Show an observed subtotal, not a complete annual footprint when coverage is partial.
4. **Add accounting and validation as an opt-in prototype.** Propose `impact/consumption.py`, `evaluation/footprint.py`, and `tests/impact/test_consumption.py`: `E_t = amount_t × mapped_multiplier_t`, reconciled by domain. Keep shared factor uncertainty systematic across transactions. Use sourced sensitivity ranges—or explicitly unquantified uncertainty—not invented confidence intervals. Only add a physical refinement if overlapping boundaries can be decomposed without double counting.

### Acceptance, payoff, effort, and limits

Require hand-computed one-to-one/weighted-map fixtures, weights summing to one, correct dimensions, currency/year incompatibility rejection, refund/transfer no-double-counting, cutoff isolation, and reconciliation of all counted/excluded/unmapped spend. Every displayed subtotal must trace to transactions, mapping, and factors; an incomplete account/year must never be labelled a complete household inventory. Existing window/missingness tests are prerequisites, not footprint tests. ([Customer360 test source](../tests/pipelines/test_customer360.py), lines 81–141, 183–205)

With reviewed reference data, report emissions/spend-weighted category errors, coverage, compatible survey-domain distributions, and paired receipt/meter bias where available. Use conventional KS maximum-CDF distance: zero means identical distributions; do not copy the reversed endpoint sentence flagged in the supplied extraction. Match population, year, currency, and household equivalization. No real-world error threshold or paper numerical reproduction is established here. ([Paper validation/limits](../research-papers/carbon-footprints-from-financial-transactions.md), lines 440–667, 751–843)

**Payoff:** a genuine consumption-domain profile could reveal hotspots and support better opportunity prioritization, distinct from observed spend propensity. **Effort:** small for scope/contract design; medium for a narrow reviewed mapping and accounting prototype; large/dependency-bound for full coverage and independent validation. Missing supplementary tables, licensed factors, household context, and representative data are blockers. Physical passports alone cannot supply transaction-footprint attribution, full lifecycle accuracy, or realized avoided emissions.

## 2. Manokhin and Grønhaug: calibration exists, but its benefits are mixed

**Paper:** Valery Manokhin and Daniel Grønhaug, *Classifier Calibration at Scale: An Empirical Study of Model-Agnostic Post-Hoc Methods*, **2026**, arXiv:2601.19944v1. **Primary:** [record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19944); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19944v1). **Supplied text:** [classifier-calibration-at-scale.md](../research-papers/classifier-calibration-at-scale.md). **Assessment source:** [calibration/trees note](../research_notes/Research%20paper%20integration%20assessment/calibration_and_trees.md).

### Actual contribution and verdict

This is an empirical study of **binary** post-hoc calibration across 21 classifiers and 30 TabArena tasks, comparing isotonic, Platt, Beta, Venn–Abers, and Pearsonify under a held-out protocol. It emphasizes proper scores and computational costs; Beta/Venn–Abers are candidates, not guaranteed winners. Some comparisons also change learner training-set size, and the study does not establish universal superiority or distribution-shift guarantees. ([Methods and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19944v1#S5.SS2))

**Verdict: partial, with real sigmoid/isotonic implementation.** Four independent product-adoption outcomes are binary tasks, not a mutually exclusive multiclass problem. Existing sigmoid/isotonic calibration is not temperature/vector/Dirichlet multiclass calibration; conversely, this supplied paper itself is not the original multiclass-calibration study. Replicating all benchmark tasks or adding multiclass methods is unnecessary for the current product formulation.

### Implemented aspects and saved evidence

`Calibrator` fits near-unregularized logistic regression on probability logits or monotone isotonic regression. It compares two-fold validation OOF Brier when both classes have at least two examples, permits automatic isotonic only with at least 50 examples **in each class**, otherwise forces Platt, refits on validation, clips outputs, and stores fitted-data-sensitive versions/reliability diagnostics. ([calibration.py](../src/greenpath/models/propensity/calibration.py), lines 25–125)

Training excludes test and the reserved confidence-release cohort, but reuses remaining validation for learner tuning/selection, calibration, and F1 threshold selection. Thresholds use in-sample calibrated validation predictions. This is not the paper's separate calibration-sample protocol, and OOF calibrator diagnostics do not remove all selection optimism. Saved selections are **LOGISTIC + Platt for all four products**, with only 10–17 validation positives, so the small-sample fallback applies throughout. ([training.py](../src/greenpath/models/propensity/training.py), lines 169–246; [release reservation](../src/greenpath/pipelines/run.py), lines 208–245; [saved JSON](./ml_evaluation.json))

| Product | Saved test n / positives | Brier raw → calibrated | ECE raw → calibrated |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| EV Finance | 346 / 28 | 0.067934 → 0.069678 | 0.019570 → 0.013510 |
| Green Home | 279 / 26 | 0.082122 → 0.082315 | 0.018885 → 0.011329 |
| Solar Finance | 354 / 24 | 0.043989 → 0.040700 | 0.042375 → 0.031196 |
| Paperless Banking | 105 / 20 | 0.155255 → 0.150128 | 0.083486 → 0.053091 |

These are selected-model values extracted by the note worker from [saved ML JSON](./ml_evaluation.json), not new runs. Lower ECE coexists with worse EV/Home Brier; therefore **“calibration improved probability quality for every product” is false for this saved comparison**. Equal-width ten-bin ECE is not the paper's monotonic equal-mass diagnostic. Log-loss, ECI, and Spiegelhalter Z are not in the inspected summary. ([Evaluation](../src/greenpath/evaluation/ml.py), lines 82–92, 198–281; [metrics](../src/greenpath/evaluation/metrics.py), lines 211–257, 295–319)

### Small staged plan

1. **Report before switching.** Add log-loss and paired raw/calibrated proper-score reporting in [metrics.py](../src/greenpath/evaluation/metrics.py), [ml.py](../src/greenpath/evaluation/ml.py), and [reports.py](../src/greenpath/evaluation/reports.py). Benchmark identity/no-calibration on the same fitted learner; publish counts, clipping, binning, and sample policy.
2. **Improve selection independence.** Within non-test/non-release customers, use separate tuning/calibration/decision-tuning partitions or cross-fit the full selection/calibration procedure. Preserve release reservation. Persist cohort identities and evaluate sparse-class tradeoffs rather than fragmenting already scarce positives blindly.
3. **Extend the existing seam conditionally.** Add identity and actual three-parameter Beta candidates to `_fit_method`, `_apply`, versioning, and serialization. Review a pinned implementation. Add Venn–Abers later as a benchmark with multiprobability outputs distinct from merged point probabilities; do not silently change [PropensityResult](../src/greenpath/contracts/propensity.py) into an interval contract. Pearsonify is lower priority.

### Acceptance, payoff, effort, and limits

Extend [propensity tests](../tests/ml/test_propensity.py), especially validation-only fitting, method fallback, monotone outputs, identity, and fitted-data versioning. Test/release mutation must not alter calibrators or thresholds. Require deterministic bounded finite outputs, sparse/single-class policy, serialization, and monotonicity only where advertised. Select using prespecified validation proper scoring; after freezing, compare paired Brier/log-loss deltas and uncertainty on untouched test, missingness, and shift cohorts. Success is an auditable comparison and safe fallback—not a forced improvement.

**Payoff:** judges see probability quality measured rather than asserted, including honest negative results. **Effort:** small for log-loss/identity reporting; medium for reviewed candidates and independence redesign. Sample scarcity and added inference/serialization complexity constrain interpretation. A merged Venn–Abers score must not inherit a perfect-calibration or individual-true-probability guarantee; merging loses the foundational theoretical property. ([Foundational Venn–Abers abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.00213))

Better adoption calibration does **not** release correctness confidence. All saved core confidence decisions remain suppressed, and any changed propensity/calibrator/threshold would require fresh downstream confidence fitting and independent release decisions in separately authorized work. ([Confidence report](./CONFIDENCE_VALIDATION.md); [confidence feature/release construction](../src/greenpath/pipelines/run.py), lines 337–371)

## 3. Lundberg et al.: local SHAP is real; global understanding is still missing

**Paper:** Scott M. Lundberg, Gabriel Erion, Hugh Chen, Alex DeGrave, Jordan M. Prutkin, Bala Nair, Ronit Katz, Jonathan Himmelfarb, Nisha Bansal, and Su-In Lee, *Explainable AI for Trees: From Local Explanations to Global Understanding*, **2019**, arXiv:1905.04610v1. **Primary:** [record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.04610); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.04610v1). **Supplied text:** [explainable-ai-for-trees-global-understanding.md](../research-papers/explainable-ai-for-trees-global-understanding.md). **Assessment source:** [calibration/trees note](../research_notes/Research%20paper%20integration%20assessment/calibration_and_trees.md).

### Actual contribution and verdict

Tree SHAP supplies exact polynomial-time Shapley attributions for a specified tree expectation game, with local accuracy, consistency, and missingness properties. The paper moves from local values to global importance, dependence/interaction analysis, explanation-space structure, and supervised loss monitoring. “Exact” does not mean causal effects or exact knowledge of correlated-feature distributions. ([Original methods](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.04610v1#S10))

**Verdict: partial, with a genuine exact Tree SHAP branch.** The saved deployed selections are all logistic, so the persisted evidence demonstrates linear SHAP—not four deployed tree explainers. Keeping a simpler selected learner is legitimate; forcing boosting merely to match the paper would weaken model-selection integrity.

### Implemented aspects and missing mechanisms

`audit_shap` calls XGBoost native `pred_contribs=True, approx_contribs=False` and obtains raw margins separately. This is exact tree-path-dependent attribution using fitted training cover. The logistic branch computes centered transformed-feature contributions against a stored training background, including missing indicators: independent/interventional linear SHAP, not the tree algorithm. Both assert `base + sum(phi) = raw_margin` within `2e-5`. ([shap_audit.py](../src/greenpath/models/propensity/shap_audit.py), lines 15–57; [background fitting](../src/greenpath/models/propensity/training.py), lines 68–69, 223–224; [XGBoost API](https://xgboost.readthedocs.io/en/release_3.1.0/python/python_api.html#xgboost.Booster.predict))

The contract explicitly declares `raw_uncalibrated_log_odds` and `explains_calibrated_probability=false`. The API returns a local audit or explicit NOT_AVAILABLE. Saved [EV](./shap/ev_finance.json), [Home](./shap/green_home.json), [Solar](./shap/solar_finance.json), and [Paperless](./shap/paperless_banking.json) audits contain 100 linear rows each; the note's read-only arithmetic found tiny raw-margin residuals. This supports persisted local additivity, not execution of current runtime bundles. ([API](../src/greenpath/extensions/api.py), lines 375–391; [export](../src/greenpath/evaluation/ml.py), lines 215–220)

Missing are global SHAP summaries, dependence/beeswarm plots, interaction tensors, explanation embeddings, and SHAP-loss monitoring. Input missingness/median/KS drift is not supervised loss attribution. Runtime reason ordering uses uncentered logistic coefficient products and omits missing indicators; those reasons are not the complete selected-model SHAP audit, particularly if boosting were selected. ([Reason contribution helper](../src/greenpath/models/propensity/training.py), lines 249–260; [provider](../src/greenpath/models/propensity/provider.py), lines 85–105; [drift](../src/greenpath/evaluation/ml.py), lines 121–141)

### Small staged plan

1. **Aggregate the actual selected-model locals.** Extend [shap_audit.py](../src/greenpath/models/propensity/shap_audit.py) and [ml.py](../src/greenpath/evaluation/ml.py) to export full or representative deterministic held-out cohorts rather than first-100 rows. Persist model/background/cohort identity, transformed-feature semantics, count/weights, mean absolute values, signed quantiles, and units.
2. **Make the audit visible.** Use existing plotting infrastructure in [reports.py](../src/greenpath/evaluation/reports.py) for a global bar chart, beeswarm/scatter, and one feature-dependence view. Retain missing indicators; any grouped display must be labelled as a display grouping, not guaranteed group-Shapley values.
3. **Add interactions only for a relevant tree.** Optionally request native `pred_interactions=True` with exact mode, batching, and explicit bias handling. Label a GBM candidate as non-deployed when logistic remains selected. Independent linear raw-margin off-diagonals are zero. Defer explanation embeddings and composed-predictor loss attribution until a specific use and observed-label protocol exist.

### Acceptance, payoff, effort, and limits

Existing test source forces logistic/boosted selections, missing inputs, persistence, and raw-margin additivity; it was not run in this assessment. Extend [test_propensity.py](../tests/ml/test_propensity.py), lines 289–306, or add `tests/ml/test_shap_audit.py`. Require global ranking recomputation from persisted locals, row-permutation invariance, intended cohort coverage, and rejection of mixed model/background/output-space aggregates. For interactions, assert symmetry, row sums equal feature SHAP, full sums equal raw margin with bias, and unused features contribute zero; use small known-tree specified-game fixtures.

**Payoff:** local-to-global plots make model behavior inspectable and help RMs/judges understand rare dominant signals and candidate differences. **Effort:** small–medium for global summaries, medium for interactions/invariant tests, large for calibrated-output or loss explanations. No new deep learner is needed for the first increment.

Nonlinear sigmoid/isotonic calibration is **not** explained by transforming each raw SHAP value independently. Log-odds values are not calibrated percentage-point changes, and predictive explanations are not causal sustainability evidence. Background choice, correlated inputs, imputation, cohort sampling, and synthetic-only patterns remain explicit limits. ([Paper transformation warning](https://arxiv.org/html/1905.04610v1#S10.SS2); [output-space boundary](../src/greenpath/models/propensity/shap_audit.py), lines 46–57)

## 4. González-González et al.: transaction explanations require a separate attribution path

**Paper:** Jaime González-González, Silvia García-Méndez, Francisco de Arriba-Pérez, Francisco J. González-Castaño, and Óscar Barba-Seara, *Explainable Automatic Industrial Carbon Footprint Estimation From Bank Transaction Classification Using Natural Language Processing*, IEEE Access 10, 126326–126338, **2022**, DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2022.3226324. **Primary:** [IEEE record](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9968254); [author full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14505v1). **Supplied text:** [explainable-carbon-footprint-estimation.md](../research-papers/explainable-carbon-footprint-estimation.md). **Assessment source:** [carbon note](../research_notes/Research%20paper%20integration%20assessment/carbon.md). The 2024 arXiv deposit date is not the journal publication year.

### Actual contribution and verdict

The paper classifies Spanish transaction archetypes into eight activity sectors using SVC/RF/LSTM alternatives. LIME selects explanatory terms; enterprise descriptors enrich merchant-name explanations; sector vocabularies and lexical/semantic similarity diagnose whether explanation and predicted sector agree. This is **semantic agreement**, not proof of classification truth or measured carbon. Its carbon formulas infer activity from payment divided by representative unit price, then multiply by a physical factor; water produces litres, not carbon. ([Methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14505v1#S4.SS2.SSS4); [activity formulas](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14505v1#S4.SS2.SSS5))

**Verdict: partial task overlap; defining transaction-XAI and amount-to-activity estimator absent.** GreenPath's recommendation prose and propensity SHAP explain other objects. The existing physical engine intentionally avoids spend-to-litres/kWh inference and should not be weakened simply to mimic the paper.

### Implemented aspects and missing mechanisms

Description normalization and supervised short-text prediction are present, but use TF-IDF logistic regression rather than the paper's Spanish preprocessing/model choices. Eight current labels are not the paper's eight sectors: flights/courier/water/private transport are not separately represented. Rule documentation and purity/support-controlled brand routes exist, but predictions expose only category, confidence, and method—not rule IDs, matched spans, signed token weights, or competing-class explanations. Rule confidence 1.0 is deterministic convention, not calibrated certainty. ([Classifier](../src/greenpath/models/transaction_classifier.py), lines 76–92, 137–245, 350–448; [categories](../src/greenpath/contracts/enums.py), lines 34–42)

No LIME output, merchant enrichment, sector-similarity validator, or transaction price-proxy estimator appears in the inspected paths. **Paper:** Liam Toran, Cory Van Der Walt, Alan Sammarone, and Alex Keller, *Scalable and Weakly Supervised Bank Transaction Classification*, **2023**, arXiv:2305.18430v2. **Primary:** [record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18430); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18430v2). **Supplied text:** [weakly-supervised-bank-transaction-classification.md](../research-papers/weakly-supervised-bank-transaction-classification.md). **Assessment source:** [transaction note](../research_notes/Research%20paper%20integration%20assessment/transaction_classification.md).

### Actual contribution and verdict

The defining pipeline groups/preprocesses transactions, applies multiple labeling functions over text/spending patterns, aggregates probabilistic weak labels, and trains a discriminative model. The reported model uses FastText text and amount/date sequences through GRUs on separate binary tasks, with a small annotated calibration/evaluation set. Its reported balanced accuracy and biased-label precision caveats do not transfer to GreenPath's flat multiclass macro F1. ([LF/label/model methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18430v2); [supplied methods](../research-papers/weakly-supervised-bank-transaction-classification.md), lines 268–389)

**Verdict: defining weak-supervision method not implemented.** GreenPath has relevant rules and a supervised learner, but no LF vote matrix, probabilistic label model, or weak-target training. A rule miss routed to a classifier is not an LF abstention supplying evidence to a label aggregator.

### Implemented aspects and material gaps

Boundary rules and a fit-derived brand dictionary assign final hard categories. Training takes descriptions and categories, not histories or soft targets. `labelled_reference` selects eligible synthetic merchants and joins `transaction_truth.true_category`; “simulated manually-labelled” describes a simulation of a labelled subset, not real human annotation or weak-label aggregation. ([Rules/inference](../src/greenpath/models/transaction_classifier.py), lines 76–109, 199–214, 329–448; [reference construction](../src/greenpath/pipelines/run.py), lines 134–190; [truth generator](../src/greenpath/data_generation/generator.py), lines 740–760)

Merchant-group benchmark splitting and fit-only dictionary/model construction are useful scaffolding. Saved synthetic results show unseen macro F1 **0.529** versus known **0.992**, 39 unseen merchants, FUEL recall **0.135**, HOME_IMPROVEMENT F1 **0**, and no unseen GAS support. These show where evaluation is weak, not real-market performance. Neither maximum fallback probability nor rule confidence 1.0 is established as calibrated. ([Evaluation split](../src/greenpath/evaluation/transaction.py), lines 83–114; [saved categorisation report](./TRANSACTION_CATEGORISATION.md), lines 3–35, 52–73)

The paper itself notes limitations in the Snorkel implementation's abstention/posterior handling. A new implementation must review correlated-LF and abstention behavior rather than copy an old limitation. LF abstention is also distinct from a final low-confidence review/abstain policy; current OTHER/0.0 on empty text is not such a policy. ([Supplied caveat](../research-papers/weakly-supervised-bank-transaction-classification.md), lines 333–349, 402–409; [current inference](../src/greenpath/models/transaction_classifier.py), lines 416–448)

### Small staged plan

1. **Build a genuine gold audit set first.** Preserve the existing merchant split and report supports over predefined seeds. Collect appropriately permitted, independently reviewed realistic descriptions; split merchants before LF/model fitting. Keep gold test labels out of rule authoring, label modeling, features, and training. Synthetic truth alone cannot demonstrate reduced annotation burden or real unseen-merchant benefit.
2. **Create explicit LF evidence.** Propose `models/transaction_weak_supervision.py`: each LF emits class evidence or ABSTAIN. Start with high-precision text patterns; add recurring amount/date patterns only from legitimate grouped histories. Persist coverage, overlap, conflict, versions, and dependency assumptions. Fit a reviewed probabilistic label model on training rows only; leave all-abstain/conflict uncertainty visible.
3. **Test label utility with a simple learner before a sequence network.** Compare label-model predictions, a text learner trained from probabilistic labels, and the frozen supervised hybrid on the same reviewed holdout. Where the current learner lacks soft-target support, use an explicit weighted class-expanded loss or a reviewed learner that minimizes soft-label cross-entropy; hard argmax distillation must be disclosed as lossy. Do not merely rename generated truth as weak labels.
4. **Defer faithful FastText/GRU histories.** A separate history-aware training path, reviewed dependencies, compute/runtime budgets, and privacy policy would be required. [Current dependencies](../pyproject.toml) do not include the paper's full deep-learning/Snorkel/FastText stack; adding it is not an assumed small change.

### Acceptance, payoff, effort, and limits

Test LF abstention, correlated/conflicting signals, all-abstain rows, deterministic labels/versioning, and strict train/merchant/test isolation. Truth/test labels must never enter LF fit or model inputs. Report LF coverage/quality and label-model versus downstream balanced accuracy for binary tasks, plus macro/per-class F1 for the multiclass adaptation. If final review thresholds are added, report coverage-risk curves and probability calibration rather than labelling raw maxima calibrated.

Predeclare a practical improvement/non-degradation gate and use merchant-cluster paired uncertainty on untouched reviewed labels. A synthetic-only pilot is legitimate if labelled that way, but cannot prove label efficiency for real bank records. Report supported categories explicitly; absent GAS holdout support cannot receive a generalization success badge. Existing [merchant split tests](../tests/ml/test_transaction_classifier.py), lines 232–279, are intended safeguards, not executed proof here.

**Payoff:** a validated approach could reduce annotation burden and improve unknown-merchant coverage, which would benefit spend-feature reliability and downstream prioritization. **Effort:** small–medium for reviewed audit/baseline readiness, medium for LFs/label modeling and simple distillation, large for faithful sequence architecture. Benefits remain conditional on realistic inputs, independent labels, LF quality, and actual comparative results.

## 10. Grinsztajn, Oyallon, and Varoquaux: use fair local comparisons, not novelty

**Paper:** Léo Grinsztajn, Edouard Oyallon, and Gaël Varoquaux, *Why do tree-based models still outperform deep learning on tabular data?*, **2022**, arXiv:2207.08815v1; NeurIPS Datasets and Benchmarks. **Primary:** [record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08815); [NeurIPS paper](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/0378c7692da36807bdec87ab043cdadc-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf); [author benchmark](https://github.com/LeoGrin/tabular-benchmark). **Supplied text:** [why-trees-outperform-deep-learning-tabular.md](../research-papers/why-trees-outperform-deep-learning-tabular.md). **Assessment source:** [synthetic/tabular note](../research_notes/Research%20paper%20integration%20assessment/synthetic_and_tabular.md).
