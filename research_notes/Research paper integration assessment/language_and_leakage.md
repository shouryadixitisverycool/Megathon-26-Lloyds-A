## 1. How faithfully does GreenPath integrate Explingo, and what would make its explanations stronger?

### Takeaway

GreenPath implements a deliberately narrower, safer adaptation: a provider may reorder a complete set of server-authored sentences, but cannot narrate arbitrary SHAP attributions. It has strong deterministic grounding/fallback mechanisms, an offline adversarial validator suite, and an optional live harness; it does **not** implement Explingo's SHAP-to-narrative payload, independent narrative-quality grader, or exemplar optimization experiment. [Provider boundary](../../src/greenpath/llm/ports.py#L17-L38); [service](../../src/greenpath/llm/service.py#L139-L282); [offline evaluator](../../src/greenpath/llm/evaluation.py#L263-L311); [Explingo §§III–VII](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05145v1#S3).

### Cited Findings

#### What the paper actually does

- **Explanation substrate and narrator:** an external XAI system produces SHAP; the narrator selects the top-N features by absolute contribution and receives sorted `(feature name, feature value, SHAP contribution)` tuples, a format description, and context describing the prediction/units. GPT-4o transforms these into free natural-language narratives; it is not asked to discover model reasoning from a score alone. [Explingo §III-A](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05145v1#S3.SS1); [local paper, lines 126–269](../../research-papers/explingo-explaining-ai-with-language-models.md#L126-L269).
- **Independent grading:** separately prompted GPT-4o grades accuracy (0/1), completeness (0/1/2), and exemplar-style fluency (0–4); conciseness is deterministic (0–4). Accuracy concerns correctness of stated values/directions, not whether every feature appears. Completeness requires every supplied feature, its value, and contribution direction; precise contribution magnitudes are application-dependent. “Independent” means a separate grading subsystem, not proof of uncorrelated errors or a different model family. [Explingo §§III-B, V-A–C](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05145v1#S5).
- **Consistency and weighting:** each LLM metric is graded five times and averaged; disagreement below four of five agreeing raises an alert (none reported). `G = 4A + 2C + F + S` scales each metric to the same maximum of four, rather than expressing a fourfold preference for accuracy over the other normalized metrics. Conciseness is four for `L ≤ F_features × L_max`, zero for `L ≥ 2 × F_features × L_max`, and linearly interpolated between. [Explingo §V and §V-D](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05145v1#S5).
- **Grader validation is limited but real:** separate sets of 50 human-labelled explanation/narrative pairs for accuracy and completeness include deliberately wrong values, flipped signs, and omissions. Final agreement is 96% for each. These small development/validation sets do not establish banking-domain validity, general human comprehension, or universal hallucination detection. [Explingo Table II, §V-A–B, Table III](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05145v1#S5.SS1).
- **Few-shot optimization:** DSPy combines H hand-written and B bootstrapped exemplars; bootstrap candidates must have perfect accuracy/completeness/fluency and conciseness at least 3.5. Evaluation covers 169 explanations, 2,197 narratives, nine exemplar styles from four underlying datasets, and 13 techniques. H=1/B=3 has the highest aggregate grade (15.034/16); extra exemplars generally improve style/length but can reduce correctness. This is evidence for measuring the tradeoff, not a mandate to use that setting in another domain. [Explingo §§VI–VII, Table IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05145v1#S6).
- **Scope of demonstrated benefit:** comparison/reference context and user studies of decision-making benefit are future work. The paper reports weaknesses in domain-sensitive grading; it does not demonstrate that narrative explanations improve real lending decisions. [Explingo §VIII-B–C](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05145v1#S8); [local discussion, lines 750–820](../../research-papers/explingo-explaining-ai-with-language-models.md#L750-L820).

### Inferences

- **Best current submission claim:** “Explingo-inspired grounded language presentation with exact-claim validation and deterministic fallback,” not “Explingo replicated,” “SHAP narratives deployed,” or “independent LLM evaluator validated.” Its safety comes principally from output restriction, not measured model intelligence. [Service reconstruction](../../src/greenpath/llm/service.py#L199-L207); [paper system](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05145v1#S3).
- **Highest-value extension is a faithful substrate before freer language.** Existing exact selected-model SHAP makes this feasible, but describing its contributions as percentage-point changes in calibrated probability would be wrong. SHAP explains only propensity, not the rule-based recommendation state, evidence Trust, eligibility, or physical carbon estimate. [SHAP semantics](../../src/greenpath/models/propensity/shap_audit.py#L17-L57); [current separate decision/impact/trust sentences](../../src/greenpath/llm/service.py#L210-L259).
- **A quality benchmark has more immediate value than installing an optimizer.** Reordering cannot reduce word count, so paper-style conciseness differences should be zero for a fixed sentence set; a blinded comparison can establish whether ordering is even useful. Domain labels and locked evaluation cases are prerequisites to interpreting any future few-shot gain. [Exact sentence-set requirement](../../src/greenpath/llm/service.py#L203-L207); [paper exemplar tradeoff](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05145v1#S7).

## 2. Which leakage/reproducibility mechanisms from Kapoor and Narayanan are integrated, and where do guarantees stop?

### Takeaway

The propensity pipeline implements substantial leakage controls: customer-level splits, future-window labels, event/availability-aware features, train-only fitted preprocessing, validation-only tuning/calibration, and reserved confidence-release selection. It is not yet a complete model-info-sheet/reproducibility argument: semantic deduplication, domain feature-legitimacy evidence, generalized chronological fit guards, sealed-test governance, deployment-matched distribution validation and statistical uncertainty remain incomplete. [Split](../../src/greenpath/pipelines/split.py); [training](../../src/greenpath/models/propensity/training.py#L139-L224); [pipeline stages](../../src/greenpath/pipelines/run.py#L192-L265); [paper §4 / Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.07048v1).

### Cited Findings

#### Paper method and applicability

- The paper surveys leakage-related failures across 17 fields affecting 329 papers, then organizes leakage into eight types: L1.1 no test set; L1.2 preprocessing on train+test; L1.3 feature selection on train+test; L1.4 duplicates; L2 illegitimate features; L3.1 temporal leakage; L3.2 dependent train/test samples; L3.3 test-distribution sampling bias. Its motivating task is **scientific inference/generalization**, not merely getting an executable pipeline or selecting a higher metric. [Original paper §§1–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.07048v1); [local taxonomy:365–500](../../research-papers/ml-leakage-and-reproducibility-crisis.md#L365-L500).
- **Model info sheets** require arguments for separation throughout fitting/preprocessing/selection, legitimacy of every feature or justified feature group, and why the test distribution supports the claimed target population. Domain expertise and dependence/time reasoning matter; a seed, model card, blacklist or automated PASS alone is insufficient. The paper's civil-war reanalysis illustrates that removing leakage can eliminate claimed complex-model advantages. GreenPath should transfer the audit method, not reproduce civil-war experiments or assume every correlated banking feature is illegitimate. [Paper model-info-sheet method](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.07048v1); [local method:590–680](../../research-papers/ml-leakage-and-reproducibility-crisis.md#L590-L680); [Appendix D:1991–2011](../../research-papers/ml-leakage-and-reproducibility-crisis.md#L1991-L2011).

#### Important implemented invariants, qualifications and test evidence

- **Reserved release cohort:** `stage_train` reserves every third customer in a seeded permutation of the initial validation cohort as `confidence_release` **before** propensity tuning/calibration/OOD/Trust/meta fitting. `train_confidence` fits the meta-model on the remaining validation customers and decides whether to surface confidence using only the reserved cohort. Held-out-test confidence scores later added by `stage_evaluate` are diagnostics, not release selection. Saved core confidence results are all suppressed; usable release counts are EV 128, Green Home 95, Solar 115, Paperless 39, so suppressing an unsupported reliability claim is a real safeguard, not a missing confidence UI feature. - **A confidence-release test is present:** `test_confidence_release_never_reads_test` spies `_confidence_rows`, verifies only `validation` and `confidence_release` are requested, and checks `test_used_for_release_selection=False`. It returns empty data and therefore exercises the insufficient-data suppression path; it does not test release membership reservation, nonempty meta fitting, or fitted-artifact invariance under adversarial release/test mutations. Do **not** report this safeguard as completely untested. [tests/trust/test_selection.py:29–42](../../tests/trust/test_selection.py#L29-L42).
